Using Software and Internet Services

Why do everything by hand when powerful tools can help? In this lesson, you'll discover how online software like Google Forms, Google Sheets, and Google Slides can help you collect, process, and present data faster, more accurately, and more collaboratively.

100 minutes Google Forms Google Sheets Google Slides

Why Use Online Tools?

Imagine asking 100 students a survey question. If you use paper, you'd have to:

  • Print 100 copies
  • Hand them out and collect them
  • Type every answer into a spreadsheet by hand
  • Calculate the totals and averages yourself

That could take hours! But with Google Forms + Google Sheets, all of that happens automatically in minutes. Your answers go straight into a spreadsheet where formulas do the calculations for you.

💡
Key idea: Online tools save time, reduce errors, and let you collaborate with classmates in real time.
The Three Main Tools

These three Google tools work together as a team. Each one handles a different stage of working with data.

📝

Google Forms

Collect data. Create surveys with different question types and share the link.

📊

Google Sheets

Process data. Use formulas to calculate totals, averages, and percentages.

🎤

Google Slides

Present findings. Add charts and text to share your results with others.

How They Connect

Here's the magic: these tools are linked automatically. Watch the workflow:

  1. Create a survey in Google Forms.
    Add questions like "How many eggs do you eat per week?" or "How do you prefer your eggs?"
  2. Share the form link with your classmates (or their parents).
    Each person opens the form on their phone, tablet, or computer and answers.
  3. Responses appear in Google Sheets automatically.
    Every time someone submits the form, a new row is added to your spreadsheet. No typing needed!
  4. Use formulas in Sheets to process the data.
    =AVERAGE(B2:B20) finds the average. =SUM(B2:B20) finds the total.
  5. Create charts and paste them into Google Slides.
    Present your findings clearly and confidently.
Handy Formulas in Google Sheets

Here are the formulas you'll use most often in Sheets:

What You Want Formula Example
Total (sum) =SUM(B2:B20) Total eggs eaten by all students
Average (mean) =AVERAGE(B2:B20) Average eggs per student
Count =COUNT(B2:B20) How many responses
Max value =MAX(B2:B20) Highest number of eggs
Min value =MIN(B2:B20) Lowest number of eggs
Percentage =B2/$B$21*100 Each response as % of total
💡
Tip: The $ symbol locks a cell. In $B$21, when you drag the formula down, it keeps pointing to B21 — perfect for percentage calculations!

Advantages and Limitations

Online tools are powerful, but it's important to know their strengths and weaknesses.

Advantages

  • Fast: Data is collected instantly
  • Accurate: No typing errors from paper
  • Automatic: Formulas calculate for you
  • Collaborative: Multiple people can work together
  • Accessible: Works on phone, tablet, or computer
  • Shareable: Send a link with one click
  • Free: Google tools are free to use

Limitations

  • Needs internet: Can't work offline easily
  • Privacy concerns: Data is stored online
  • Learning curve: Takes time to learn formulas
  • Relies on technology: Power cuts or wifi issues can interrupt
  • Not everyone has access: Some classmates might not have devices

Activity — Design a Google Form

Your task is to design a Google Form survey about egg consumption in your school.

Your form should include:

  1. A clear title and description.
  2. At least 5 questions, including:
    • A short-answer question (for a number, like "How many eggs per week?")
    • A multiple-choice question (like "How do you prefer your eggs?")
    • A question with a linear scale (1–5, for opinions)
  3. A clean, colorful design (Google Forms has themes!).
  4. Click "Send" and copy the link — you'll share it with classmates.

Bonus: Once responses come in, open them in Google Sheets and use =AVERAGE() to find the average number of eggs eaten per week!

Key Takeaway

Online tools like Google Forms, Google Sheets, and Google Slides make working with data faster, easier, and more accurate. Forms collect data, Sheets process it with formulas, and Slides present it beautifully. But remember — online tools have limitations too, like needing internet access and raising privacy concerns.
