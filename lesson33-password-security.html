Identity Management and Password Security

Your passwords are the keys to your digital life. If someone gets them, they can pretend to be you, read your messages, and even steal your money. In this lesson, you'll learn how to create strong passwords, use passphrases, and manage your accounts like a pro.

Why Do Passwords Matter?

Every account you have — email, social media, school platform, games — is protected by a password. Think of it as the key to your digital house.

If someone gets your password, they can:

  • Read your private messages
  • Post things pretending to be you
  • Access your email or bank account
  • Steal your personal information
  • Damage your reputation with others
💡
Key idea: Your passwords are the first line of defense for your digital identity. A weak password is like leaving your front door unlocked.
What Makes a Password Strong?

Not all passwords are equal. Some are easy to guess — others are nearly impossible. Here are the 5 rules for a strong password:

  1. Make it long.
    At least 12 characters. Longer passwords are much harder to crack.
  2. Mix types of characters.
    Use uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols. Example: A, a, 4, @.
  3. Avoid personal information.
    Don't use your name, birthday, pet's name, or address — hackers can find these online.
  4. Avoid dictionary words.
    Common words like "password" or "football" are easy for computers to guess.
  5. Never reuse passwords.
    If one account is hacked, reused passwords let hackers break into all your accounts.

The Power of Passphrases

A passphrase is a password made of several random words strung together. They're longer than normal passwords, easier to remember, and much harder to crack.

Passphrase Example
correct-horse-battery-staple

This passphrase is 28 characters long, uses only lowercase letters, and would take a computer trillions of years to guess. Yet it's easy for you to remember as a mental image of a strange scene.

🎨

Make It Visual

Picture a funny scene in your mind. The weirder the image, the easier it is to remember.

🔢

Add Numbers & Symbols

Swap some letters: "h0rse" instead of "horse", or add !@# at the end.

🎭

Personal but Not Public

Use words only meaningful to you — never something a stranger could guess from your social media.

🚫

Never Reuse

Even the strongest passphrase should be used for one account only.

Identity Management

Identity management means controlling your accounts and credentials — knowing what accounts you have, keeping them secure, and being able to recover them if something goes wrong.

Here's what good identity management looks like:

  1. Keep a list of your accounts.
    You probably have more than you think. Write them down (in a safe place) — email, social media, games, school platforms.
  2. Use a unique password for each account.
    If one gets hacked, the others stay safe.
  3. Use a password manager (optional).
    Tools like Bitwarden or Google Password Manager store your passwords securely, so you only need to remember one.
  4. Keep your recovery email and phone number updated.
    If you forget your password, this is how you get back in.
  5. Log out of shared computers.
    Always log out on library, school, or shared computers. Don't leave your account open.
  6. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) when available.
    This adds a second step (like a code sent to your phone) that makes it much harder for hackers.
What To Do If Your Account Is Hacked

If you suspect your account has been hacked, act fast. Follow these 5 steps:

  1. Change your password immediately.
    Do this from a device you trust. Use a new, strong password.
  2. Check your recovery email and phone.
    Make sure they haven't been changed by the hacker.
  3. Log out of all devices.
    Most platforms have a "Log out of all devices" option in settings.
  4. Tell friends and family.
    Hackers often send scam messages to your contacts. Warn them so they don't fall for it.
  5. Report it to the platform.
    Use the "Report" feature to tell the platform you've been hacked.
💡
Be prepared: Write down your account recovery information before you need it. It saves a lot of panic.

Activity — Create a Password Policy Poster

Work with a partner to design a Password Policy poster for your school or family. Your poster should include:

  1. A title like "Our Strong Password Rules".
  2. At least 5 rules for creating strong passwords (draw from this lesson).
  3. A "Never" list — things not to do (never reuse, never share, never write them on your desk).
  4. 3 example passphrases you created as a pair. Make them memorable and unique.
  5. A what to do if hacked mini-section with 3 quick steps.

Present your poster to the class. Then save it as a reminder to stick near your home computer.

  4. Why is it dangerous to reuse the same password on multiple accounts?
  5. What is identity management? Name three practices of good identity management.
  6. What should you do first if your account is hacked? Why?

Key Takeaway

Your passwords are the keys to your digital life. Make them long, unique, and strong. Use passphrases to remember them easily, and never reuse a password across different accounts. Practice good identity management by keeping track of your accounts, using recovery options, and acting fast if something goes wrong. Homework

Part 1 — Change one password:

  • Choose one account you use regularly.
  • Change its password to a strong passphrase you create yourself.
  • Write it down in a safe place (not on your computer or phone's Notes app).
  • Tell your parents or guardian which account you changed (not the password itself!).

Part 2 — Account inventory:

  • List all the online accounts you can remember (email, social media, games, school, etc.).
  • Next to each account, write:
    • Do you use a unique password for it?
    • Is the account still active?
  • If you find accounts you don't use anymore, consider deleting them.
