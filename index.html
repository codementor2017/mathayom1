Midterm · Indicator 1 — Abstraction and Algorithm Design

10 lessons
🔍 Lesson 1

What is Abstraction?

Define abstraction as focusing on what matters for a goal. Identify essential and non-essential details in a messy room.

100 min Open
📋 Lesson 2

Essential vs Non-Essential Details

Use criteria to decide whether a detail is essential. Sort details into groups and explain how removing non-essential details clarifies a problem.

100 min Open
✏️ Lesson 3

Problem Statement and Purpose

Write a clear problem statement with goal, input, output, and constraints. Identify purpose before choosing details.

100 min Open
👣 Lesson 4

Algorithms in Daily Life

Explain that an algorithm is a clear sequence of steps. Represent a daily process as an ordered algorithm and identify abstraction in routines.

100 min Open
📊 Lesson 5

Pseudocode and Flowcharts

Use simple pseudocode to describe an algorithm. Draw a basic flowchart with start/end, process, decision, and arrows.

100 min Open
📏 Lesson 6

Variables, Constants, and Units

Identify variables and constants in a problem. Choose appropriate units and explain why only needed data should be used.

100 min Open
🌱 Lesson 7

Case Study: Turf-Laying Problem

Solve the turf problem by identifying essential details. Calculate area and number of turf pieces, and round up when needed.

100 min Open
⚙️ Lesson 8

Designing the Turf Algorithm

Create an algorithm for the turf problem. Write pseudocode and draw a flowchart. Test the algorithm with different inputs.

100 min Open
🐛 Lesson 9

Testing, Edge Cases, and Improvement

Test an algorithm with edge cases. Identify assumptions and errors. Improve the algorithm using feedback.

100 min Open
🏆 Lesson 10

Final Project: Real-Life Algorithm with Abstraction

Apply abstraction to a real-life problem. Design a complete algorithm with pseudocode or flowchart. Present and justify essential vs non-essential details.

100 min Open
