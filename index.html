Midterm · Indicator 1 — Abstraction and Algorithm Design

10 lessons
🍯 Lesson 1

What is Abstraction?

Define abstraction as focusing on what matters for a goal. Identify essential and non-essential details in a messy room.

100 min Open
🐻 Lesson 2

Essential vs Non-Essential Details

Use criteria to decide whether a detail is essential. Sort details into groups and explain how removing non-essential details clarifies a problem.

100 min Open
🌳 Lesson 3

Problem Statement and Purpose

Write a clear problem statement with goal, input, output, and constraints. Identify purpose before choosing details.

100 min Open
🍃 Lesson 4

Algorithms in Daily Life

Explain that an algorithm is a clear sequence of steps. Represent a daily process as an ordered algorithm and identify abstraction in routines.

100 min Open
🐝 Lesson 5

Pseudocode and Flowcharts

Use simple pseudocode to describe an algorithm. Draw a basic flowchart with start/end, process, decision, and arrows.

100 min Open
🌲 Lesson 6

Variables, Constants, and Units

Identify variables and constants in a problem. Choose appropriate units and explain why only needed data should be used.

100 min Open
🌿 Lesson 7

Case Study: Turf-Laying Problem

Solve the turf problem by identifying essential details. Calculate area and number of turf pieces, and round up when needed.

100 min Open
🏡 Lesson 8

Designing the Turf Algorithm

Create an algorithm for the turf problem. Write pseudocode and draw a flowchart. Test the algorithm with different inputs.

100 min Open
🔍 Lesson 9

Testing, Edge Cases, and Improvement

Test an algorithm with edge cases. Identify assumptions and errors. Improve the algorithm using feedback.

100 min Open
🏆 Lesson 10

Final Project: Real-Life Algorithm with Abstraction

Apply abstraction to a real-life problem. Design a complete algorithm with pseudocode or flowchart. Present and justify essential vs non-essential details.

100 min Open
🎉
Midterm Complete! You finished all 10 lessons of Indicator 1. Take a break — the Final period of Semester 1 is next.

Final · Indicator 2 — Simple Programming for Math/Science

10 lessons
💻 Lesson 11

Introduction to Programming and Algorithms

Explain what a program and an algorithm are. Identify math/science problems that can be solved by a program.

100 min Open
📦 Lesson 12

Variables and Data Types

Define variables and data types (integer, float, string). Use variables in a simple Python program and choose appropriate types.

100 min Open
🔀 Lesson 13

Conditions (if/else) for Decision Making

Use if, elif, and else in Python. Write programs that make decisions based on math/science data.

100 min Open
🔁 Lesson 14

Loops (for/while) for Repetition

Use for and while loops in Python. Write programs that repeat calculations and apply loops to math/science problems.

100 min Open
🧩 Lesson 15

Step-by-Step Problem Solving

Break a complex problem into smaller steps. Write an algorithm before coding and use abstraction to choose essential steps.

100 min Open
🚀 Lesson 16

Motion Equation Program

Use the motion equation s = ut + ½at² in a program. Apply variables, conditions, and loops. Test and debug the program.

100 min Open
📐 Lesson 17

Area Calculation Program

Write programs to calculate areas of different shapes. Use conditions to choose the shape and test with different values.

100 min Open
⚖️ Lesson 18

BMI Calculation Program

Write a BMI calculator program. Use variables and conditions. Interpret BMI categories.

100 min Open
🔧 Lesson 19

Testing and Debugging Simple Programs

Test a program with normal inputs. Identify and fix common errors. Improve a program using feedback.

100 min Open
🏅 Lesson 20

Final Project: Simple Program for Math/Science

Design and write a complete simple program to solve a math/science problem. Use variables, conditions, and loops.

100 min Open

Midterm · Indicator 3 — Collecting, Processing, and Presenting Data

10 lessons
📊 Lesson 21

Introduction to Data and Primary Sources

Define data, information, and primary data. Distinguish primary data from secondary data and identify primary sources.

100 min Open
📝 Lesson 22

Methods of Collecting Primary Data

Use surveys, interviews, observations, and measurements to collect data. Design good survey questions and record accurately.

100 min Open
🧮 Lesson 23

Organizing and Processing Data

Organize raw data into tables. Calculate totals, averages, and percentages. Explain why processing makes data meaningful.

100 min Open
⚖️ Lesson 24

Creating Alternatives and Evaluating Data

Create alternative solutions from processed data. Evaluate alternatives using simple criteria and choose the best with evidence.

100 min Open
📈 Lesson 25

Introduction to Data Presentation

Choose appropriate chart types for different data. Create charts using spreadsheet software and explain why visualization helps.

100 min Open
🛠️ Lesson 26

Using Software and Internet Services

Use software and online tools for collecting, processing, and presenting data. Use Google Forms, Google Sheets, and presentation tools.

100 min Open
🥚 Lesson 27

Case Study: Boiling Eggs

Integrate science and data to solve a real problem. Collect data on egg boiling preferences and present findings.

100 min Open
🏘️ Lesson 28

Case Study: BMI of Local People

Collect height and weight data from a sample. Calculate BMI and categorize results. Analyze trends in the local community.

100 min Open
📉 Lesson 29

Graphs from Experiments and Trends

Record experimental data accurately. Create line graphs from experimental results. Analyze trends and make predictions.

100 min Open
🏆 Lesson 30

Final Project: Collect, Process, Evaluate, Present Data

Design a complete data project from collection to presentation. Use software and online tools throughout the process.

100 min Open

Final · Indicator 4 — Safe and Responsible IT Use

10 lessons
🛡️ Lesson 31

Introduction to Safe and Responsible IT Use

Define safe and responsible use of information technology. Identify risks of unsafe IT use and explain why digital citizenship matters.

100 min Open
🔒 Lesson 32

Protecting Privacy and Identity Online

Define privacy, personal data, and digital identity. Identify what information should be kept private and explain risks of oversharing.

100 min Open
🔑 Lesson 33

Identity Management and Password Security

100 min Open
🎣 Lesson 34

Protecting Personal Data and Avoiding Scams

100 min Open
✅ Lesson 35

Evaluating the Appropriateness of Content

Define inappropriate content. Identify privacy violations, obscenity, and rude criticism. Explain the impact of harmful content.

100 min Open
💬 Lesson 36

Cyberbullying and Respectful Online Communication

100 min Open
📜 Lesson 37

100 min Open

100 min Open
