Final Project: Real-Life Algorithm with Abstraction

This is your chance to put everything together! You'll choose a real-life problem, use abstraction to focus on what matters, design a complete algorithm with pseudocode or a flowchart, test it, and present your work to the class.

Project Overview

Your mission is to design an algorithm for a real-life problem. Along the way, you'll use everything you've learned in this unit:

🔍

Abstraction

Focus on the essential details. Ignore the rest.

📝

Problem Statement

Write a clear statement with goal, input, output, and constraints.

📋

Algorithm Design

Write the steps in the correct order.

💻

Pseudocode or Flowchart

Present your algorithm in a clear format.

🧪

Testing

Test with different inputs, including edge cases.

🎤

Presentation

Explain your work clearly to the class.

💡
Key idea: This project isn't just about solving a problem — it's about showing your thinking. You'll explain why you chose the details you did.

Step 1 — Plan Your Project

Before you start designing, plan carefully. Write your plan with these 4 parts:

  1. Problem statement.
    Write your problem using the formula: "We need to ___ using ___ to produce ___ while ___."
  2. Essential details.
    List at least 5 essential details for your problem.
  3. Non-essential details.
    List at least 5 non-essential details you'll ignore.
  4. Algorithm plan.
    Will you use pseudocode, a flowchart, or both?
💡
Get approval first. Show your plan to your teacher before you start designing.
Step 2 — Design Your Algorithm

Now design your algorithm. It must include:

💻

Pseudocode OR Flowchart

Write your algorithm in one (or both) of these formats.

📋

Clear Steps

Use START and END. Include INPUT, calculations, and PRINT.

🎯

Variables

Use variables so your algorithm works for different inputs.

⏫

Rounding (if needed)

Include ROUND UP if your answer needs whole numbers.

💡
Tip: If your algorithm has a decision (like "if it's raining"), use a diamond in the flowchart and IF/ELSE in the pseudocode.
Image 3 [Algorithm Design] — A student designing an algorithm with pseudocode on the left and a flowchart on the right. Focused, creative illustration. Recommended size: 900 × 400 px.
Step 3 — Test Your Algorithm

Test your algorithm with at least two examples. One should be a normal case, and one should be an edge case.

Create a small debugging log like this:

Test Input Expected Actual Fix
Normal case ___ ___ ___ ___
Edge case ___ ___ ___ ___
💡
Did your algorithm break? That's okay — that's what testing is for! Fix the bug and test again.
Step 4 — Present Your Work

Your presentation should last 5 minutes. Include these 5 parts:

  1. Problem statement.
    What problem are you solving? Why does it matter?
  2. Essential vs non-essential details.
    What did you focus on? What did you ignore? Why?
  3. Your algorithm.
    Show your pseudocode or flowchart. Explain the steps.
  4. Your test results.
    Show what happened when you tested it.
  5. How abstraction helped.
    Explain how focusing on essential details made your problem easier to solve.
💡
Presentation tips: Speak clearly. Point at your work when explaining. Be ready to answer one question from the class.

Project Rubric

Your teacher will use this rubric to assess your project. Review it carefully before you present.

Criteria Excellent (4) Good (3) Fair (2) Needs Work (1)
Problem Statement Clear goal, input, output, constraints Missing one part Missing two parts Unclear or missing
Essential Details 5+ correct essential details 4 correct 3 correct Fewer than 3
Non-Essential Details 5+ correctly ignored 4 correct 3 correct Fewer than 3
Algorithm Correctness Logical, complete, tested Mostly correct Some errors Major errors
Clarity Easy to follow Understandable Some confusion Confusing
Real-Life Connection Strong and practical Good Weak Unrealistic
Presentation Clear, confident, engaging Clear Somewhat clear Difficult to follow
Reflection Insightful and detailed Good Basic Missing

Key Takeaway

In this unit, you've learned to use abstraction to focus on what matters, write clear problem statements, design algorithms with pseudocode and flowcharts, and test and improve your work. Your final project brings all of these skills together. Remember: a great algorithm isn't just about what you do — it's about why you do it, and being able to explain it clearly.
