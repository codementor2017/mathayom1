Introduction to Safe and Responsible IT Use

The internet is an amazing place — but it also has risks. In this lesson, you'll learn what it means to be a safe and responsible digital citizen. You'll explore the four big areas of online safety: privacy, identity, content, and agreements — and see how your choices online can protect or harm you and others.

How Do You Use IT?

Think about your last 24 hours. What technology did you use? Maybe:

  • Messaged friends on your phone
  • Scrolled through social media
  • Watched videos on YouTube
  • Played online games
  • Used a school platform for homework

Now here's an honest question: Did anything feel unsafe or uncomfortable? Maybe someone asked for personal information. Maybe you saw a comment that felt mean. Maybe you clicked a link you weren't sure about.

💡
Key idea: Technology is powerful. Whether it helps or hurts us depends on how we use it. That's what this unit is all about.
What is Safe IT Use?

Safe IT use means protecting yourself, your data, and others while using technology. It's not just about avoiding bad things — it's also about making good choices that help everyone have a positive online experience.

There are four big areas of safe and responsible IT use. Let's look at each one:

🔒

Privacy

Keeping your personal information private and safe. Don't share your address, phone number, or passwords online.

🪪

Identity

Managing who you are online. Use strong passwords, be careful with your digital footprint, and don't let others pretend to be you.

📱

Content

Judging whether content is appropriate. Avoid harmful, hateful, or dangerous material and report it when you see it.

📜

Agreements

Following the rules and terms of the platforms you use — like copyright, licenses, and terms of service.

Examples of Unsafe Use

Let's look at some common ways people are unsafe online. Recognizing these risks helps you avoid them.

Unsafe Behaviour Why It's Risky The Safer Choice
Sharing your home address online Strangers can find you in real life Never share your address publicly
Using "123456" as a password Extremely easy to guess — accounts get hacked Use strong passwords with 12+ characters
Posting rude comments Hurts others and damages your reputation Think before you type — be respectful
Using someone's photo without permission Violates their privacy and copyright Always ask first, or use Creative Commons
Clicking links from unknown senders Can steal your personal data (phishing) Never click — report and delete
Sharing passwords with friends Anyone can access your account as you Keep passwords private — always
💡
Consequences of unsafe use: Identity theft, cyberbullying, account hacking, loss of trust, and even legal problems. Being safe online protects your future.

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship means being a responsible, respectful, and safe member of the online world. Just like being a good citizen in your town or school, being a good digital citizen means:

  1. Respecting others. Treat people online the way you want to be treated.
  2. Protecting yourself. Keep your data and identity safe.
  3. Following the rules. Obey laws, copyrights, and platform terms.
  4. Being honest. Don't spread false information or pretend to be someone else.
  5. Helping others. Stand up for people who are being bullied online.
💡
Remember the Golden Rule of the Internet: If you wouldn't say it or do it in person, don't say it or do it online either.

Your Turn — Safe or Unsafe?

Read each of these 10 scenarios. Decide whether each is Safe or Unsafe, and explain why in your notebook.

  1. Using the same password for all accounts.
  2. Posting your school name and photo publicly.
  3. Downloading a song from an illegal site.
  4. Asking permission before posting a friend's photo.
  5. Sharing your home address in a game chat.
  6. Using a Creative Commons image with attribution.
  7. Sending rude messages to a classmate.
  8. Reporting a suspicious email.
  9. Clicking a link from an unknown sender.
  10. Logging out of a shared computer.
💡
Hint: Ask yourself: Does it protect privacy? Does it respect others? Does it follow the rules?

Check Your Understanding

Answer these questions in your notebook:

  1. What does "safe IT use" mean?
  2. Name the four big areas of safe and responsible IT use.
  3. Give two examples of unsafe online behaviour and explain why each is risky.
  4. What is digital citizenship? Name three qualities of a good digital citizen.
  5. Why is it important to protect your identity online?
  6. What does the Golden Rule of the Internet mean?

Key Takeaway

Being safe and responsible online means protecting your privacy, managing your identity, judging content wisely, and following agreements. As a digital citizen, your choices affect you and everyone around you. Next lesson, you'll dive deeper into protecting your privacy and identity online — a skill you'll use every single day.

Homework

Part 1 — Reflect on your own IT use:

  • Write down three IT activities you did today.
  • Label each as Safe or Unsafe.
  • For any unsafe activity, write what you would do differently next time.

Part 2 — Family conversation:

  • Ask a family member: "Have you ever received a scam or phishing message? How did you respond?"
  • Write a short paragraph about what you learned.
