Case Study: Turf-Laying Problem

It's time to put everything together! In this lesson, you'll solve a real-world problem: how many pieces of turf do we need to cover a lawn? You'll use abstraction to focus on the essential details, area formulas to calculate, and rounding to give a practical answer.

100 minutes Area Calculation Unit Conversion Rounding Up

Warm-Up: Area Review

Before we start, let's review the area formula for a rectangle:

Area Formula
Area = length × width

Quick example: A rectangle is 4 m long and 3 m wide. What is its area?

💡
Answer: Area = 4 m × 3 m = 12 m²

What unit do we use for area? We use square units — like cm², m², or km². That's because area is 2-dimensional (length × length).

Presenting the Turf Problem

Here's our problem:

🌱
"You want to lay turf in a lawn. One piece of turf is 50 cm wide and 50 cm long. How many pieces do you need?"

Wait — is this enough information? No! We're missing something important.

What information is missing? The size of the lawn!

Now, let's add it: "The lawn is 5 m long and 4 m wide."

What are the essential details?

✅

Essential

  • Lawn size: 5 m × 4 m
  • Turf piece size: 50 cm × 50 cm
  • Shape: rectangle
  • Units: m and cm
❌

Non-Essential

  • Color of the grass
  • Brand of the turf
  • Weather on the day
  • Who plants it
💡
Abstraction in action: We focus only on what matters for the problem — the sizes and units. Everything else is non-essential.

Guided Solve — Step by Step

Let's solve the problem step by step. We'll use the 5-step method:

  1. Convert units.
    The lawn is in meters. The turf is in centimeters. Convert them all to meters.
    50 cm = 0.5 m
    So one turf piece is 0.5 m × 0.5 m.
  2. Calculate lawn area.
    Area = length × width = 5 m × 4 m = 20 m²
  3. Calculate one turf piece area.
    Area = length × width = 0.5 m × 0.5 m = 0.25 m²
  4. Divide to find the number of pieces.
    Number of pieces = Lawn area ÷ Piece area
    = 20 ÷ 0.25 = 80 pieces
  5. Round up if needed.
    In this example, 80 is a whole number, so no rounding needed.
    Answer: 80 pieces of turf.
Summary Formula
Pieces needed = Lawn area ÷ Turf piece area

Rounding Up — Why and When?

Sometimes the answer is not a whole number. For example, what if the lawn was 5.1 m × 4 m?

  1. Convert: 50 cm = 0.5 m
  2. Lawn area: 5.1 m × 4 m = 20.4 m²
  3. Piece area: 0.5 m × 0.5 m = 0.25 m²
  4. Divide: 20.4 ÷ 0.25 = 81.6 pieces
  5. Round up! We can't buy 81.6 pieces. We need 82 pieces.
💡
Why round up? We can't buy a part of a turf piece. If we round down to 81, the lawn won't be fully covered. So we always round up — even if the decimal is small.

Examples of rounding up:

  • 81.6 → 82 pieces
  • 80.1 → 81 pieces
  • 80.9 → 81 pieces
  • 80.0 → 80 pieces (already a whole number)

Common Mistake: Forgetting to Convert

One of the most common mistakes is forgetting to convert units. Let's see what happens if we forget:

Wrong approach:

  • Lawn area: 5 m × 4 m = 20 m²
  • Turf piece area: 50 cm × 50 cm = 2,500 cm²
  • Divide: 20 ÷ 2,500 = 0.008 pieces 🤔

That answer makes no sense! Why? Because we mixed m² with cm². You can't divide 20 m² by 2,500 cm² — those are different units!

Correct approach: Always convert to the same unit first.

💡
Golden rule: Units are essential. Always check that all measurements are in the same unit before calculating.

Key Takeaway

To solve the turf problem (and many real-world problems), follow these 5 steps: 1) Convert all units, 2) calculate the total area, 3) calculate the piece area, 4) divide, and 5) round up if needed. Remember: units are essential — always convert before calculating. And when the answer isn't a whole number, always round up because you can't buy a partial piece.
