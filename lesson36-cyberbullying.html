Cyberbullying and Respectful Online Communication

Words hurt — especially online, where they can be shared with hundreds of people in seconds. In this lesson, you'll learn what cyberbullying is, how it affects people, and how you can be an upstander — someone who stands up for others instead of watching silently.

Words Can Hurt

Imagine you're scrolling through your phone and you see this: A classmate has received 50 mean messages in one night. The messages say things like:

💔
"Nobody likes you."
"You're so ugly."
"Just leave our school."
"Everyone is laughing at you."

How do you think that person feels? Afraid? Alone? Ashamed?

Now here's the key question: What would you do if you saw this happening?

💡
Key idea: Cyberbullying isn't just "mean comments." It's a repeated attack on someone's dignity, safety, and mental health. And every one of us has the power to stop it.
What is Cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is repeated harmful behavior that happens online or through digital devices. It's meant to hurt, scare, embarrass, or exclude someone.

It's different from regular bullying because:

  • It can happen 24/7 — even at home
  • It can reach a huge audience instantly
  • It's often anonymous — the bully can hide behind a screen
  • The evidence stays online forever
💡
Important: A single mean comment is bad — but cyberbullying is when it happens repeatedly and is intended to hurt.
Forms of Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying takes many forms. Here are the 5 most common:

💬

Harassment

Sending repeated mean messages to someone. Example: Sending 30 angry texts to a classmate.

🎣

Trolling

Posting comments on purpose to provoke someone into getting angry or upset.

🚫

Exclusion

Leaving someone out on purpose. Example: Creating a group chat and not inviting a friend.

🎭

Impersonation

Pretending to be someone else online to embarrass them or damage their reputation.

📸

Outing

Sharing someone's secrets or private information without their permission.

😡

Flaming

Using angry, insulting language in online arguments. Often starts small and escalates quickly.

👻

Cyberstalking

Following someone online with unwanted messages, tracking, or threats. Very serious.

🤐

Doxxing

Publishing someone's private information (address, phone, photos) publicly to endanger them.

The Impact of Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying isn't just "hurt feelings" — it has serious effects on real people:

Effect On What Happens
Mental Health Anxiety, depression, loneliness, low self-esteem, panic attacks
School Life Lower grades, skipping school, trouble concentrating
Social Life Isolation, fear of making friends, broken trust
Physical Health Trouble sleeping, headaches, stomach aches, fatigue
In Serious Cases Self-harm, or even thoughts of suicide
💡
If you or someone you know is experiencing serious distress, please reach out to a trusted adult, school counselor, or a helpline immediately. You are not alone, and help is always available.

The Four Roles

In every cyberbullying situation, there are four roles. Which one will you play?

😠

The Bully

The person who does the harm. They send mean messages, post embarrassing content, or exclude someone on purpose.

😢

The Target

The person who receives the harm. They may feel alone, scared, or ashamed — even if they did nothing wrong.

😐

The Bystander

Someone who sees it happening but does nothing. Bystanders don't cause harm directly, but staying silent lets it continue.

🦸

The Upstander

Someone who sees it and acts to help. Upstanders make the biggest difference — they support the target and stand up for what's right.

💡
Key idea: Bystanders and upstanders both see the same thing. The only difference is what they choose to do. Being an upstander takes courage — but it changes lives.

How to Be an Upstander

Being an upstander doesn't mean being a hero or fighting anyone. It means taking small, safe actions that make a big difference. Here are 6 upstander moves you can use:

  1. Don't share or like the harmful post.
    Every like or share makes it spread further. Starve it of attention.
  2. Support the target privately.
    Send a simple message: "I saw what happened. You don't deserve that. I'm here for you."
  3. Speak up publicly — if it's safe.
    You can comment: "That's not cool. Stop." But only if you feel safe.
  4. Screenshot the evidence.
    Save proof before the post is deleted. This helps if you need to report it.
  5. Report to the platform.
    Use the "Report" button. Most platforms have strict rules against bullying.
  6. Tell a trusted adult.
    If someone is being hurt badly, don't handle it alone. Tell a parent, teacher, or counselor.
💡
Not sure what to say? Try these: "I'm here for you." "That wasn't okay." "Want to talk?" "You matter."
Respectful Online Communication

The best way to prevent cyberbullying is to be respectful in the first place. Here are 6 rules for respectful communication online:

  1. Think before you type.
    Once you hit send, you can't take it back.
  2. Remember there's a person on the other side.
    Behind every screen is a real human being with real feelings.
  3. Use the Three-Question Test.
    Is it respectful? Is it true? Is it helpful?
  4. Don't use ALL CAPS to yell.
    Texting in all caps feels aggressive and angry.
  5. Avoid sarcasm in text.
    Sarcasm is hard to read — it often comes across as mean.
  6. Apologize when you make a mistake.
    Everyone messes up. A sincere apology goes a long way.

Your Turn — Identify the Roles

Read this scenario carefully. Then answer the questions below in your notebook.

📱
The Scenario:
A student posts a mean meme about a classmate in a group chat. 20 students like it. 5 students share it to other groups. 2 students message the target to check on them. 3 students report the post to the chat admin. The rest of the students say nothing.

Answer these questions:

  1. Who is the bully?
  2. Who is the target?
  3. Who are the bystanders?
  4. Who are the upstanders?
  5. What should the bystanders have done differently?
  6. What impact do you think this had on the target?

Activity — Create an Upstander Action Plan

Work in a group of 3–4 students. Together, create an Upstander Action Plan for your school or class.

Your plan should include:

  1. How to recognize cyberbullying when you see it. (List at least 3 signs.)
  2. How to support the target privately. (Write 3 example messages you could send.)
  3. How to report safely — to the platform, to a teacher, and to a parent.
  4. How to be respectful online — 5 rules everyone can follow.
  5. A slogan or motto for your campaign. Example: "Kindness is free. Share it!"

Present your plan as a poster, a slide deck, a short skit, or a class pledge. Hang it in your classroom as a reminder.

Check Your Understanding

Answer these questions in your notebook:

  1. What is cyberbullying? How is it different from regular bullying?
  2. Name three forms of cyberbullying with a short definition of each.
  3. What is the difference between a bystander and an upstander?
  4. Name three effects of cyberbullying on the target.
  5. What are three things you can do to support someone being bullied?
  6. Why is it important to tell a trusted adult if you see serious cyberbullying?

Key Takeaway

Cyberbullying is real, painful, and preventable. Your choices online can either hurt someone or help them. Be an upstander — don't share the harmful post, support the target, report it, and tell a trusted adult. And remember: respectful communication is the best prevention. Homework

Part 1 — A kind message:

  • Think of someone who might need a kind word — a classmate, friend, or family member.
  • Send them a genuine, supportive message today.
  • Write a short reflection: How did it feel to send it? What was their reaction?

Part 2 — Research a real case:

  • Find one news story about cyberbullying (from a trusted source).
  • Write a short summary (3–4 sentences) answering:
    • What happened?
    • Who was affected?
    • What could have prevented it?

Part 3 — Reflect:

  • Write a short paragraph: "What kind of digital citizen will I be?"
  • Include one promise you're making to yourself about how you'll treat others online.
