Final Project: Digital Citizenship Guide

This is your chance to teach others what you've learned! You'll create a Digital Citizenship Guide for students your age — covering everything from passwords and privacy to cyberbullying and Creative Commons. Your guide will help make the online world a safer, kinder place for everyone.

100 minutes Create a Guide Design & Present Teach Others

Project Overview

Your mission is to create a Digital Citizenship Guide that teaches other students how to be safe, respectful, and responsible online. Think of it as a handbook or friendly guide that you would want a younger student to read.

Your guide should cover at least 5 topics from this unit:

🔒

Privacy & Identity

Protecting personal data and digital footprints.

🔑

Password Security

Strong passwords and passphrases.

🎣

Scams & Phishing

Spotting and responding to scams.

✅

Appropriate Content

Judging content and respecting others.

💬

Cyberbullying

Standing up and supporting others.

📜

Terms of Use

Reading the fine print of apps.

🎨

Creative Commons

Using media legally and respectfully.

📚

Plagiarism

Citing sources and academic honesty.

💡
Key idea: Your guide should be useful, clear, and engaging. Make it something a student would actually want to read.
Choosing Your Format

You can create your guide in the format that works best for you. Here are some options:

📄

Poster

A large, colorful poster with sections, images, and short tips.

📊

Slide Deck

A presentation (Google Slides, PowerPoint) with one topic per slide.

🎥

Short Video

A 3–5 minute video where you present your guide visually and narrate it.

📖

Brochure / Booklet

A folded brochure or small booklet with tips and illustrations.

💡
Whatever format you choose, make sure it's easy to read, has clear headings, and uses images or icons to make it visually appealing.

What to Include

Your guide must include the following 5 required elements:

  1. Cover at least 5 topics.
    Choose 5 (or more) topics from this unit that you want to teach others about.
  2. Use at least 2 Creative Commons images.
    Add images with proper attribution (Title, Author, Source, License).
  3. Include practical tips.
    Give 3–5 tips per topic. Make them concrete and easy to follow.
  4. Include a "What to do if something goes wrong" section.
    Give steps for what to do if someone is hacked, bullied, or sees something inappropriate.
  5. Include a conclusion or call to action.
    End with a positive message encouraging others to be good digital citizens.
💡
Extra credit (optional): Include a section on digital wellbeing — balancing screen time, taking breaks, and protecting mental health.

Step-by-Step Project Plan

Follow these 6 steps to complete your project in the time you have:

  1. Choose your topics (10 min).
    Pick at least 5 topics from the unit. Write them down.
  2. Choose your format (5 min).
    Poster, slides, video, or brochure? Choose one and commit.
  3. Plan your content (15 min).
    For each topic, write:
    • A short definition
    • 3–5 practical tips
    • One example
  4. Find CC images (10 min).
    Find at least 2 Creative Commons images that match your topics. Record the TASL information for each (Title, Author, Source, License).
  5. Create your guide (30 min).
    Design your poster, slides, video script, or brochure. Keep it clean, colorful, and easy to read.
  6. Prepare your presentation (10 min).
    Plan a 4–5 minute presentation of your guide. Practise it once.
💡
Stay focused. This project is designed for one class period of 100 minutes. Prioritize quality over quantity — 5 solid topics are better than 8 rushed ones.
Presentation Checklist

When you present your guide to the class, make sure you cover these 5 things:

Element What to Say
1. Introduction What is your guide about, and who is it for?
2. Topics Covered Name your 5 topics and give one key tip for each.
3. CC Images Show your images and explain how you gave proper attribution.
4. What to Do If... Share your emergency response section (hacked, bullied, etc.).
5. Conclusion End with your call to action — what should your audience do next?
💡
Speak clearly. Make eye contact. Point at your images. Don't read from notes — explain your ideas in your own words.
