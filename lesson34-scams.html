Protecting Personal Data and Avoiding Scams

"Congratulations! You've won 50,000 baht!" — Have you ever seen a message like that? It's probably a scam. Scammers are clever and they try to trick you into giving away your personal data or money. In this lesson, you'll learn how to spot the signs of scams and how to respond safely.

Have You Seen This?

Imagine you receive this message on your phone:

📱
"🎉 CONGRATULATIONS! You have won 50,000 baht! Click this link to claim your prize NOW before it expires in 24 hours!"

What would you do? Would you click the link? Would you give your personal information?

💡
The truth: This is a classic phishing scam. There is no prize. The scammer wants you to click the link so they can steal your data or install malware on your device.
Common Types of Scams

Scammers use different methods to trick you. Here are the most common ones:

🎣

Phishing

Fake emails that look real — they ask you to "log in" or "confirm your account." The page is fake and steals your password.

📱

Smishing

Phishing via SMS text messages. Usually says your "package is delayed" or you "won a prize."

📞

Vishing

Phishing via phone calls. The caller pretends to be from your bank or a government office.

🌐

Fake Websites

Websites that look almost identical to real ones — but they steal your login details.

🦠

Malware

Harmful software that gets installed when you click a bad link or download a fake app. Includes viruses, spyware, and ransomware.

💸

Fake Shopping

Online stores that take your money but never send the product. Very common on social media ads.

❤️

Romance Scams

Strangers who pretend to be your friend or romantic interest to gain your trust, then ask for money.

💼

Job Scams

"Easy work from home — earn 5,000 baht/day!" Usually asks you to pay a "registration fee" first.

Warning Signs of a Scam

Scammers are good at sounding believable. But they always leave clues. Learn these 6 warning signs:

Warning Sign Example
🚨 Urgent language "ACT NOW!" or "Expires in 24 hours!" or "Your account will be locked!"
❌ Spelling & grammar errors Messages full of typos that a real company wouldn't make
❓ Unknown sender An email or message from someone you don't know
🔑 Asks for password or money "Please confirm your password" or "Send 500 baht to unlock your prize"
🔗 Suspicious links Links that look wrong, like amaz0n.com instead of amazon.com
🎁 Too good to be true "You won 1 million baht!" or "Get a free iPhone!"
💡
Golden rule: If it feels suspicious — it probably is. Never click, never reply, always report.

How Scammers Steal Your Data

Scammers have many tricks. Here are the most common ways they get your data:

  1. Fake login pages.
    The link takes you to a page that looks identical to a real login page, but it's controlled by the scammer. When you type your password, they capture it.
  2. Malicious downloads.
    You download a "free" app or file, but it contains malware that spies on you.
  3. Data breaches.
    A legitimate company you use gets hacked, and your data leaks online. This is why you should use different passwords everywhere.
  4. Social engineering.
    The scammer pretends to be someone you trust — a friend, teacher, or family member — to trick you into giving data.
  5. Fake prize or refund offers.
    "You've won!" or "You're owed a refund!" — all they need is your bank info to "send" the money.
How to Respond Safely

If you suspect a scam, follow these 5 steps. It's the same every time:

🚫

1. Do NOT Click

Don't click any links in the message. Don't open attachments.

💬

2. Do NOT Reply

Replying confirms your number or email is active — you'll get more scams.

🚩

3. Report It

Use the "Report" or "Mark as Spam" button in your email or messaging app.

⛔

4. Block the Sender

Block the number or email address so you don't hear from them again.

🗑️
5. Delete the Message. Once you've reported and blocked, delete the message to keep your inbox clean and avoid accidentally clicking later.

Activity — Create a Scam Awareness Guide

Work with a partner to create a one-page guide that teaches others how to spot and avoid scams.

Your guide must include:

  1. A clear title — something catchy like "Don't Take the Bait!" or "Spot the Scam!"
  2. A short definition of what a scam is (in your own words).
  3. 5 warning signs — with a small icon for each.
  4. 5 safe responses — what to do if you spot a scam.
  5. A section on who to report to:
    • A trusted adult (parent, teacher)
    • The platform (email, app, etc.)
    • In serious cases — the police (in Thailand: 1441 hotline)
  6. One example scam message you write yourself, with the warning signs labeled.

Design your guide visually. You can include drawings, colors, and icons. Present it to the class.

Check Your Understanding

Answer these questions in your notebook:

  1. What is phishing? How is it different from smishing and vishing?
  2. Name three warning signs that a message might be a scam.
  3. Why is "urgent language" a common trick used by scammers?
  4. What are the 5 safe response steps if you receive a suspicious message?
  5. What is social engineering? Why is it dangerous?
  6. Why should you never give your password to someone who asks for it — even if they claim to be from your bank?

Key Takeaway

Scammers use urgency, fear, and greed to trick you. But you can outsmart them by remembering: never click, never reply, always report, always block, and always delete. If something feels "too good to be true" — it is. Trust your instincts and always ask a trusted adult if you're unsure. Next lesson, you'll learn how to evaluate the appropriateness of content — another essential skill for staying safe online!

Homework

Part 1 — Family conversation:

  • Ask a family member: "Have you ever received a scam message? What did you do?"
  • Write a short paragraph about what you learned.

Part 2 — Scam analysis:

  • Find a real example of a scam message online (screenshot or copy the text — never click the links).
  • Identify the warning signs in it.
  • Write down what you would do if you received it.

Part 3 — Spread the word:

  • Share one scam warning tip with a friend or family member who might not know about it.
  • Write one sentence about their reaction.
