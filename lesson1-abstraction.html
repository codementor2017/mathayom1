What is Abstraction?

When you're solving a problem, not every detail matters. Some details are essential — they help you reach your goal. Others are non-essential — they're interesting, but they don't help you solve the problem. In this lesson, you'll learn how to focus on what matters and ignore what doesn't. That skill is called abstraction.

Warm-Up: The Messy Room

Imagine you walk into a very messy room. On the floor and furniture, you see:

  • Clothes scattered everywhere
  • Books piled on the desk
  • Toys on the bed
  • A closed door on one wall
  • A window on another wall
  • A clock ticking on the wall
  • A lamp glowing in the corner
  • A cat sleeping on a chair
  • A cup of water on the nightstand
  • A phone buzzing on the bed
  • A pair of shoes near the door

Now imagine your goal is: "Find the exit as fast as possible."

Which details matter for this goal? Which don't?

💡
Key idea: The details that matter depend on the goal. If your goal changes, the important details change too.
What is Abstraction?
Definition
Abstraction = focusing on important details
and ignoring unimportant ones for a specific goal.

Abstraction does NOT mean removing everything. It means choosing the right details for the problem you're trying to solve.

Example 1 — Telling a friend how to walk to the school gate

  • Essential: "Turn left, walk 100 meters, cross the road."
  • Non-essential: The color of the houses, the names of the shops, the weather.

Example 2 — Buying a birthday cake

  • Essential: Number of people, flavor, budget, date.
  • Non-essential: The color of the box, the name of the baker, the music playing in the shop.
💡
Why is abstraction useful? It makes problems simpler, clearer, and easier to solve.

Guided Practice: Packing a School Bag

Scenario: You are packing your school bag for a swimming lesson.

Here are possible items you could bring:

  • Swimsuit
  • Towel
  • Goggles
  • Shampoo
  • Homework
  • Pencil case
  • Lunch box
  • Phone
  • Book
  • Hat
  • Sunscreen
  • Flip-flops

Let's sort them:

Essential (for swimming) Non-Essential (for swimming)
Swimsuit Homework
Towel Pencil case
Goggles Book
Shampoo Lunch box
Flip-flops Phone

Now change the goal: "Pack for a full school day with swimming after class."

What happens? Now homework, pencil case, and lunch box become essential too!

💡
Key idea: The goal decides what is essential. Change the goal, and the important details change too.

Group Activity: Robot Tour Guide

Work in groups of 3–4 students.

Your task: A robot needs to travel from our classroom to the library. Write step-by-step instructions for the robot. The robot only understands clear directions. Include only essential details.

Remember: The robot cannot understand:

  • "Walk a bit"
  • "Go past the pretty tree"
  • "Turn when you feel like it"

The robot CAN understand:

  • "Walk 5 meters forward"
  • "Turn left 90 degrees"
  • "Stop at the red door"
💡
Think about it: Does the robot need to know the color of the walls? Does it need to know who painted them? Why or why not?

Your Turn — Identify the Details

For each goal below, write 3 essential details and 3 non-essential details in your notebook.

  1. Goal: Prepare for a test tomorrow
  2. Goal: Cook rice for dinner
  3. Goal: Find a book in the library
  4. Goal: Choose a birthday present for a friend
💡
Ask yourself: "Does this detail help me reach my goal? If I removed it, would the goal still be clear?"

Activity — Change the Goal

Choose one scenario below. Write down what's essential for both goals, and explain how the important details changed when the goal changed.

🎒

Scenario A

Goal 1: Pack for a school day.
Goal 2: Pack for a beach trip.

🍳

Scenario B

Goal 1: Cook breakfast for yourself.
Goal 2: Cook breakfast for 10 people.

📚

Scenario C

Goal 1: Study for a math test.
Goal 2: Study for a history test.

🛒

Scenario D

Goal 1: Buy snacks for yourself.
Goal 2: Buy snacks for a class party.

For your chosen scenario, answer:

  1. What are the essential details for Goal 1?
  2. What are the essential details for Goal 2?
  3. Which details are different between the two goals? Why?
  4. Which details stayed the same? Why?

Check Your Understanding

Answer these questions in your notebook:

  1. What is abstraction? Write your own definition.
  2. Does abstraction mean "removing everything"? Explain why or why not.
  3. Why do different goals need different details?
  4. Give one example of a detail that is essential in one situation but non-essential in another.
  5. Why is abstraction useful when solving problems?
  6. What would happen if you tried to solve a problem while focusing on every detail?

Key Takeaway

Abstraction means focusing on what matters for a specific goal and ignoring what doesn't. The goal decides what's essential — and if the goal changes, the important details change too. Abstraction makes problems simpler, clearer, and easier to solve. Next lesson, you'll learn how to use criteria to decide whether a detail is truly essential or not.

Homework

Find one real-life situation at home (for example: cooking rice, washing dishes, going to the market).

  • Write down 5 essential details for that situation.
  • Write down 5 non-essential details.
  • Write one sentence explaining your goal.
  • Then reflect: "Why did I choose these details as essential?"
