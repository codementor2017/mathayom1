Protecting Privacy and Identity Online

Every time you go online, you leave a trail of information behind you. Some of it is safe to share, but some of it should stay private forever. In this lesson, you'll learn the difference between public and private information, how your digital footprint works, and how to protect your identity online.

What is Personal Data?

Personal data (also called personal information) is any information that can identify you or be used to contact you. It's yours — and you have the right to decide who sees it.

Here are some examples:

  • Your full name
  • Your home address
  • Your phone number
  • Your birthday
  • Your school name
  • Your email address
  • Your photos
  • Your passwords
  • Your family details
  • Your national ID number
💡
Think of it this way: If a stranger knowing this information could find you, contact you, or pretend to be you — it's personal data.
Public vs Friends-Only vs Private

Not all information needs to be kept secret. Some things are fine to share publicly. The key is knowing which is which. Think of it as three levels:

Public Friends Only Private (Never Share)
Your favourite colour Photos with friends Your full home address
Your hobbies Your school name Your passwords
Your favourite food Your class schedule Your ID number
A nickname A group chat with friends Your bank details
A drawing you made A photo you took of a project Your email password
💡
Golden rule: When in doubt, keep it private. You can always share more later — but once something is online, you can't take it back.

Digital Identity and Footprint

Your digital identity is who you are online — your usernames, your posts, your comments, your photos, and your behavior across all platforms.

Your digital footprint is the trail of data you leave behind as you use the internet. Every post, like, comment, search, and click adds to it.

📸

What You Post

Photos, videos, comments, and messages you create. These are permanent — even if you delete them.

👣

What Others Post

When friends tag you or mention you, they add to your footprint — sometimes without asking.

🔍

What Gets Tracked

Websites and apps track what you search, watch, and buy. This creates a profile of you.

🎓

Who Can See It

Schools, employers, and even future friends can look you up. Your footprint follows you forever.

💡
Remember: The internet has a long memory. Even "deleted" posts can be saved in screenshots or archives. Always think before you post.

Risks of Oversharing

Oversharing means sharing more personal information than you should. It's one of the most common mistakes people make online — and the consequences can be serious.

What You Share Who Might Misuse It The Risk
Your school and class schedule Strangers, stalkers They know where to find you
Photos with your house number Thieves, strangers They know where you live
Your birthday and pet's name Hackers They can guess your passwords
Vacation photos while away Burglars They know your house is empty
Angry or emotional posts Anyone (forever) Damages your reputation permanently
Your live location Anyone watching They know exactly where you are
💡
Remember: You can't "un-share" something online. Once it's out, it's out. Always ask yourself: "Who could see this? What could they do with it?"
How to Protect Your Privacy

Here are 6 practical steps you can take today to protect your privacy online:

  1. Check your privacy settings.
    On every social media app, go to Settings → Privacy and set your account to Friends Only or Private. Review who can see your posts, tag you, and message you.
  2. Use a nickname or first name only.
    You don't need to use your full name online. A nickname is safer and just as fun.
  3. Never share your location in real time.
    Turn off location sharing on posts, stories, and apps unless you really need it.
  4. Think before you post.
    Ask yourself: "Will I be happy with this in 5 years?" If the answer is "no," don't post it.
  5. Ask before posting about others.
    Your friends have the right to privacy too. Always ask permission before posting photos or info about them.
  6. Regularly review your digital footprint.
    Search your own name online. Look at your old posts. Clean up anything you wouldn't want a teacher or future employer to see.

Activity — Digital Footprint Audit

Imagine a fictional student named Alex. Here's what Alex does online:

📸

Alex's Habit 1

Posts daily selfies with a public account.

🏫

Alex's Habit 2

Shares school name, class schedule, and teacher names.

🔑

Alex's Habit 3

Uses the same username and password everywhere.

👥

Alex's Habit 4

In your notebook, answer these questions:

  1. What are the risks in Alex's digital footprint?
  2. Which habit is the most dangerous? Why?
  3. Write 5 improvements Alex could make to be safer online.
  4. What could Alex's digital footprint look like to a future employer?

  5. Name three steps you can take to protect your privacy online.
  6. Why should you ask permission before posting about your friends?

Key Takeaway

Your privacy and identity are two of your most valuable possessions online. You have the right to control who sees your personal data. Remember: think before you share, check your privacy settings, and review your digital footprint regularly. Homework

Part 1 — Audit your own accounts:

  • Open one social media account you use.
  • Go to Settings → Privacy.
  • Write down what you find:
    • Who can see your posts?
    • Who can send you messages?
    • Who can tag you?
  • Change any setting you're not comfortable with.
  • Write one paragraph explaining what you changed and why.

Part 2 — Digital footprint check:

  • Search your own name online.
  • Write down what you find (public posts, tagged photos, mentions).
  • Reflect: Is there anything you'd want to remove? Why?
